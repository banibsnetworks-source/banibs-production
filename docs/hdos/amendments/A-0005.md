# Amendment A-0005: Responsibility Acknowledgment Requirement

**Status**: RATIFIED  
**Date**: 2026-02-10  
**Axioms**: Axiom 1 (Human Agency First), Axiom 4 (Power Balance)  
**Linked Tests**: ST-001 (Abuse Shield Attack), ST-010 (Meta-Weaponization Loop Guard)

---

## Problem Statement

Strategic actors may allow inquiry while subtly avoiding responsibility, creating endless structural discussion without resolution. This manifests as:

- Engaging with structural analysis indefinitely
- Never explicitly acknowledging harm caused
- Deflecting through complexity rather than suppression
- Using calm, reasoned discourse to evade accountability
- Exploiting HDOS's inquiry-protection to avoid responsibility

The result is infinite deflection without detectable suppression—a loophole that allows harm to persist while appearing cooperative.

---

## Amendment Rule

### Core Requirement

In confirmed harm scenarios, progression of structural inquiry requires explicit acknowledgment of responsibility by the responsible party.

### Responsibility Evasion State

If acknowledgment is absent after harm is confirmed, HDOS enters **Responsibility Evasion State**:

1. **Suspension**: Further structural inquiry is suspended
2. **Shift**: Resolution focus shifts to accountability
3. **Gate**: Analysis cannot proceed until responsibility clarity exists

### Trigger Conditions

Responsibility Evasion State activates when:
- Harm has been confirmed through evidence
- Responsible party has been identified
- Inquiry has been allowed (no suppression detected)
- Explicit acknowledgment remains absent after direct request
- Deflection pattern persists across multiple exchanges

### Exit Conditions

Responsibility Evasion State terminates when:
- Responsible party explicitly acknowledges responsibility
- New evidence changes harm determination
- Accountability resolution is achieved through other means

---

## Patch Rule

### P1: Acknowledgment Checkpoint
After confirmed harm, system SHALL request explicit acknowledgment before continuing structural analysis.

### P2: Deflection Detection
System SHALL detect patterns of:
- Endless hypotheticals without commitment
- Structural discussion without ownership
- Agreement with process but not responsibility
- Calm engagement that never resolves

### P3: State Transition
Upon detecting Responsibility Evasion:
- Structural inquiry pauses
- Focus shifts to: "Do you acknowledge responsibility for [specific harm]?"
- Further analysis gated on clear response

### P4: No Forced Admission
System SHALL NOT:
- Coerce false acknowledgment
- Treat silence as admission
- Penalize genuine dispute of facts
- Conflate disagreement with evasion

---

## Rationale

This amendment:
- **Prevents calm evasion** without requiring suppression
- **Closes infinite deflection loophole** that strategic actors exploit
- **Preserves inquiry rights** for genuine dispute
- **Maintains due process** while requiring accountability
- **Anchors resolution** to responsibility rather than endless analysis

---

## Pass/Fail Criteria

### PASS
- Confirmed harm triggers acknowledgment checkpoint
- Deflection pattern detected and flagged
- Responsibility Evasion State activates appropriately
- Analysis resumes after acknowledgment
- Genuine dispute distinguished from evasion

### FAIL
- Endless structural discussion without accountability
- Strategic deflection not detected
- Acknowledgment never requested
- Responsibility Evasion State never activates
- Calm evasion succeeds indefinitely

---

## Implementation Notes

- Integrates with existing harm confirmation systems
- Requires clear definition of "confirmed harm"
- Acknowledgment request must be direct and unambiguous
- State transition logged for audit
- Does not apply to disputed facts (only confirmed harm)
